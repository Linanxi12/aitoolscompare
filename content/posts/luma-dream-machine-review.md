## What Luma Dream Machine Does

Luma AI launched Dream Machine in mid-2024 and iterated through Ray 1.5/1.6 before Ray 2 became one of the most-used video generators in 2025-2026. It generates video two ways:

- **Text-to-video** — describe a scene, get a 5-second clip (720p/1080p)
- **Image-to-video** — animate a still image, extend a clip, or create loops
- **Keyframes** — provide start/end frames and let the model fill the motion between them
- **Camera control** — pan, zoom, orbit, dolly prompts that actually work
- **Loops and extensions** — extend clips, create seamless video loops for backgrounds

## 3 Key Tests

### Test 1: Photorealistic B-Roll
**Prompt:** "Drone shot flying over a coastal highway at sunrise, ocean on the right, morning mist, cinematic." **Result:** The most believable output of any tool tested — mist, reflections, and lighting all physically consistent. The camera move read as genuine drone footage. Side-by-side with Sora, most viewers picked Luma's clip as "real" more often. This is its core strength: scenes that look filmed.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Photorealism is Luma's defining edge — best b-roll generator available.</strong></p></div>

### Test 2: Motion Physics — People in Action
**Prompt:** "A woman jogging through a park, leaves blowing, autumn afternoon." **Result:** Runner's motion is weighty and natural — foot strikes, arm swing, hair movement all correct. At 5 seconds, no limb-warping appeared. However, a more complex prompt ("two people fencing, close combat") showed the classic AI-video failure: swords and arms briefly melted into each other. Fast, complex action remains the category-wide weakness.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Simple human motion: excellent. Complex action: still breaks.</strong></p></div>

### Test 3: Consistency and Stitching
**Task:** Generate a 20-second scene by extending a 5-second clip four times. **Result:** Each extension stayed reasonably consistent, but lighting and camera angle drifted by the fourth segment — visible seams. For social clips under 10 seconds: no problem. For anything longer: you'll notice the joins. Keyframes mode helped: providing explicit start/end frames tightened the middle two segments substantially.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Excellent for short clips; long scenes need manual stitching.</strong></p></div>
