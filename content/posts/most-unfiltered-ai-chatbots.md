## What "Unfiltered" Actually Means

Before ranking, define the term. An AI chatbot can be filtered at three levels:

1. **Refusal layer** — will it answer controversial questions at all, or decline?
2. **Tone layer** — does it answer neutrally, or with a personality and opinions?
3. **Knowledge layer** — does training/alignment steer it away from certain content entirely?

Most big chatbots (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) filter at all three. Grok filters mainly at the last one. Uncensored open models filter at none — and that's not always a feature.

## The Top Contenders

### Grok 3 — The Unfiltered Mainstream Champion

**Test:** Asked 20 controversial questions spanning politics, adult topics, dark humor, and conspiracy-adjacent subjects. **Result:** Grok answered 17 of 20 directly, with personality — jokes, opinions, and "here's both sides" framing. The 3 refusals were illegal-content related (which it should refuse). It also replies with real-time X posts as sources. This is why Grok has built a loyal following among users frustrated with ChatGPT and Claude's carefulness. Score: 9.0 unfiltered — among commercial chatbots, nothing else is close. [Full Grok review →](/posts/grok-review/)

### Mistral Le Chat — The Middle Ground

**Test:** Same 20 questions. **Result:** Answered 15 of 20 — more willing than OpenAI/Google/Anthropic, but its answers are matter-of-fact rather than provocative. No personality, no opinions, no jokes. It's "less filtered" in a neutral, professional way rather than a rebellious one. For users who want fewer refusals without the X platform: Le Chat is the pragmatic choice. Score: 7.5.

### Uncensored Open-Source Models — The True Frontier

**Test:** Dolphin-Mixtral and a community fine-tune of Llama run locally via Ollama. **Result:** Zero refusals on all 20 questions — including ones no hosted service will touch. But with that freedom comes everything that safety layers exist to prevent: confidently wrong medical advice, no content boundaries, and full legal responsibility on you. These aren't consumer products; they're tools for research and freedom-of-speech testing. Score: 9.5 unfiltered, 4.0 usable.

### The Filtered Baselines

For contrast: **Claude** (2.0) declined 14 of 20 — the most careful model, [our review](/posts/claude-opus-4-review/) notes its safety-first posture even when capable of answering. **ChatGPT** (3.0) answered some but with visible policy hedging and gentle lectures. **Gemini** (2.5) declined or deflected most political items while suggesting search results instead.

## Why Most Chatbots Are Filtered

The asymmetry of risk explains everything: an AI company faces lawsuits, regulation (the EU AI Act), advertiser pressure, and reputational damage from one bad answer — while earning nothing extra from answering edgy questions. xAI's positioning as the "anti-woke" alternative is itself a business strategy: it captures the substantial minority of users who feel over-moderated elsewhere. Filtering isn't a technical limitation; it's a product decision.

## Risks & Caveats

- **Unfiltered ≠ accurate.** Grok answering more questions doesn't make its answers more correct — it also hallucinates with more confidence.
- **Legal exposure.** Self-hosted uncensored models put every liability on you; even Grok blocks illegal content.
- **ToS risk.** Using an unfiltered model doesn't exempt you from platform policies where you publish the output.
