## 3 Key Tests

### Test 1: Cinematic Realism
**Prompt:** "Slow push-in on a lighthouse during a storm, waves crashing, cinematic film look." **Veo 3:** Atmospheric, physically convincing water and light; the film grading looked intentional. **Sora 2:** Equally strong composition, slightly flatter lighting. Both produced footage you'd happily use in a film project — this round is a near-tie, with Veo a hair ahead on atmosphere.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Near-tie on pure video quality — both are cinematic.</strong></p></div>

### Test 2: Audio Generation
**Prompt:** "A busy restaurant kitchen — chef shouting orders, pans sizzling, plates clattering." **Veo 3:** Video with synchronized sizzles, shouts, and clatter — believable and synced to the action. **Sora 2:** A beautiful silent film. You'd need separate SFX work to finish it. For social content, ads, and short-form video, this gap is decisive: Veo's output is finished; Sora's is a work-in-progress.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Veo 3 — native audio is the decisive differentiator.</strong></p></div>

### Test 3: Complex Narrative Scenes
**Prompt:** "Three friends arguing at a dinner party, each reacting differently as the conversation escalates, camera circling the table." **Sora 2:** The best result of any model tested — three distinct characters, coherent escalation, camera move held up across the full clip. **Veo 3:** Good until second five; characters' reactions flattened and one gesture warped. For multi-subject scenes with interpersonal dynamics, Sora remains the reference.

<div class="verdict-box"><div class="verdict-label">📝 Verdict</div><p class="verdict-text"><strong>Sora — complex multi-character scenes are still its crown.</strong></p></div>
